PatternForge AI — Success Metrics

Philosophy of Measurement
PatternForge measures what it claims to improve. The platform's core promise is improving thinking ability, not problem count. Therefore the primary success metrics are cognitive and behavioral, not volumetric. We do track engagement and retention for business health, but we never confuse engagement with learning.

Tier 1 — Core Learning Metrics (Primary)
These are the metrics that directly measure whether PatternForge is delivering on its core promise.

Metric 1: Pattern Recognition Accuracy Rate
Definition: Percentage of problems on which the user correctly identifies the optimal algorithmic pattern during the Pattern Discovery phase before receiving any clues.
Measurement: (Correct first-attempt pattern identifications) / (Total pattern discovery attempts) × 100
Target Benchmarks:

Onboarding baseline: expected 30–45% for most users
4-week target: ≥55%
8-week target: ≥70%
12-week target: ≥80%

Why It Matters: This is the single most direct measure of whether the platform is training the skill it claims to train. A user who improves from 35% to 78% has fundamentally changed how they process problem statements.

Metric 2: Observation Quality Score
Definition: AI-generated score (0–100) measuring the completeness, relevance, and depth of observations written during the Observation Training phase, averaged across all problems in a rolling 7-day window.
Measurement: Average of per-problem observation scores, weighted slightly toward recent sessions.
Target Benchmarks:

Onboarding baseline: expected 30–50 for most users
4-week target: ≥55
8-week target: ≥68
12-week target: ≥78

Why It Matters: Observation quality is the upstream skill that determines pattern identification and optimization ability. Improving this score is the single highest-leverage intervention PatternForge can make.

Metric 3: Optimization Discovery Rate
Definition: Percentage of problems where the user independently transitions from a working solution to a more optimal one (in terms of time or space complexity) without being prompted by a hint.
Measurement: (Problems where user reached a better solution after initial submission, without using hints) / (Total problems submitted with suboptimal first solution) × 100
Target Benchmarks:

Onboarding baseline: expected 15–25%
4-week target: ≥35%
8-week target: ≥50%
12-week target: ≥65%

Why It Matters: Most users stop at the first working solution. The ability to push further toward optimal is a direct interview differentiator.

Metric 4: Formula Discovery Success Rate
Definition: Percentage of Formula Discovery Trainer exercises where the user correctly derives the relationship, recurrence, or invariant independently (without revealing the answer).
Measurement: (Independent correct derivations) / (Total Formula Discovery exercises attempted) × 100
Target Benchmarks:

Onboarding baseline: expected 20–35%
4-week target: ≥45%
8-week target: ≥60%
12-week target: ≥72%


Metric 5: Interview Simulation Score Trend
Definition: Average score across all Interview Simulation Arena sessions in a rolling 30-day window, tracked week over week.
Measurement: Composite score (0–100) built from pattern identification accuracy (30%), observation completeness (25%), complexity achieved (25%), and edge case coverage (20%) per simulation.
Target Benchmarks:

After first 4 simulations: baseline established
8-week target: ≥15-point improvement over baseline
12-week target: ≥25-point improvement over baseline

Why It Matters: This is the closest proxy to real interview performance the platform can generate.

Metric 6: Reflection Quality Score
Definition: AI-generated quality score (0–100) for the four reflection prompts completed after each problem, measuring specificity, depth, and insight quality.
Measurement: Average reflection score across all problems in a rolling 7-day window.
Target Benchmarks:

Early users expected to score 40–55 (shallow, generic reflections)
4-week target: ≥60
8-week target: ≥72

Why It Matters: Reflection quality is a leading indicator of long-term retention. Users who reflect deeply retain patterns significantly longer, reducing re-learning overhead.

Tier 2 — Learning Progress Metrics (Secondary)
These metrics support the primary metrics and provide granular visibility into the user's growth trajectory.

Metric 7: Dimensional Skill Score Movement
Definition: Change in each of the six dimensional scores (Pattern Recognition, Observation Quality, Formula Discovery, Optimization Ability, Speed, Consistency) from assessment baseline over time.
Measurement: Recalculated every 2 weeks based on performance across all problem attempts.
Target: Every user should show measurable improvement (≥8 points) in their declared weakest dimension within 6 weeks.

Metric 8: Mistake Category Distribution Shift
Definition: Change in the distribution of error types over time. Specifically, tracking whether high-level errors (Observation Errors, Pattern Errors) decrease in proportion as the user improves.
Measurement: Weekly Mistake Report data aggregated monthly. Success = proportion of Observation and Pattern Errors decreasing; Implementation Errors may increase proportionally as users attempt harder problems.
Why It Matters: A user who starts with 60% Observation Errors and ends with 15% Observation Errors has fundamentally changed their approach. A user whose error distribution stays constant is not improving their thinking process regardless of how many problems they solve.

Metric 9: Time-to-Pattern Identification
Definition: Average time (in minutes) from problem opening to correct pattern identification, tracked over time.
Measurement: Logged automatically from the time the problem opens to the time a correct pattern is confirmed.
Target: Reduction of 30–40% from baseline over 8 weeks.
Why It Matters: Interview speed is a real constraint. Pattern recognition must become fast, not just accurate.

Metric 10: Pattern Library Growth Quality
Definition: Number of Pattern Cards generated per week, weighted by the reflection quality score of each card.
Measurement: (Cards generated this week) × (Average reflection quality of those cards / 100)
Target: 5–8 high-quality Pattern Cards per week for an active user.
Why It Matters: Library size without quality is just data hoarding. This metric ensures the library is actually useful for interview revision.

Tier 3 — Product Health and Engagement Metrics (Operational)
These metrics measure platform health and business viability. They are necessary but not sufficient — high engagement with low learning improvement is a failure state.

Metric 11: Daily Active Users (DAU) and Weekly Active Users (WAU)
Target (6 months post-launch): 1,000 DAU, 4,500 WAU

Metric 12: Day-7 Retention Rate
Definition: Percentage of users who return to the platform within 7 days of registration.
Target: ≥45% (industry benchmark for edtech is 25–30%; PatternForge targets above average due to the structured daily challenge creating a habit loop)

Metric 13: Day-30 Retention Rate
Target: ≥28%
Why This Is Hard: Most users drop off within 2 weeks. Day-30 retention above 28% indicates the platform has created genuine habit and perceived value.

Metric 14: Daily Challenge Completion Rate
Definition: Percentage of active users who complete all four daily challenge problems (with reflection) on a given day.
Target: ≥55% of DAU

Metric 15: Pattern Discovery Phase Engagement Rate
Definition: Percentage of problem attempts where the user provides a genuine multi-sentence pattern hypothesis rather than clicking through minimally.
Target: ≥80% (if this drops below 70%, it indicates users are gaming the gate rather than engaging with it — a product design problem requiring intervention)

Metric 16: AI Mentor Interaction Quality
Definition: Percentage of AI Mentor recommendations that the user acts on within the next session (i.e., if the Mentor recommends a graph observation drill, does the user actually engage with it?).
Target: ≥60% action rate on Mentor recommendations
Why It Matters: If users are ignoring Mentor recommendations, either the recommendations are not relevant or the trust in the system has not been established.

Tier 4 — Outcome Metrics (Long-Term Validation)
These are the ultimate proof that PatternForge works. They are harder to measure but most important for validating the platform's existence.

Metric 17: Self-Reported Interview Success Rate
Definition: Among users who complete 8+ weeks on PatternForge and then report taking a product company interview, what percentage report passing the technical round?
Measurement: Post-interview survey sent automatically 30 days after a user marks their interview date in their profile.
Target: ≥55% technical round pass rate (industry average for well-prepared candidates targeting product companies is estimated at 25–35%)

Metric 18: Improvement Over Previous Interview Performance
Definition: Among users who had failed a technical round before joining PatternForge and subsequently passed one after using the platform for 8+ weeks, what percentage attribute the improvement to PatternForge?
Measurement: Survey-based, with attribution question.
Target: ≥70% attribution among successful switchers

Metric 19: Net Promoter Score (NPS)
Definition: Standard NPS survey — "How likely are you to recommend PatternForge AI to a friend preparing for product company interviews?" (0–10 scale)
Measurement: Sent at Day 14, Day 45, and Day 90.
Target: NPS ≥ 50 at Day 45 (extremely strong for an edtech product)

Metric 20: Pattern Recognition Score vs Interview Outcome Correlation
Definition: As the platform accumulates data, we track whether users with Pattern Recognition Accuracy above 75% are statistically more likely to pass technical interviews than users below 50%.
Why It Matters: This is the validation metric that proves PatternForge's core thesis — that improving pattern recognition accuracy, not problem count, predicts interview success. If a strong correlation exists, it becomes the platform's most powerful marketing proof point and the foundation for future product development decisions.
